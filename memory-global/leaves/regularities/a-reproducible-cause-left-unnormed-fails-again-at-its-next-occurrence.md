# a-reproducible-cause-left-unnormed-fails-again-at-its-next-occurrence

## Regularity

An activity is constituted by its reproduction: when the cause of a difficulty is reproducible and the norm governing the activity is left unchanged, the next occurrence of the activity fails the same way. A one-off cause does not recur, so leaving its norm unchanged costs nothing.

## Generality

Level 1 — ranges over difficulties in repeated activities that a governing norm (an instruction, a gate, a habit) regulates. It is deliberately not claimed for causes that genuinely will not recur, nor for the durability of any particular way of recording the re-norming.

## Induced from

- [[recording-experience]] — contributes the observed practice behind the statement: at difficulty closure a reproducible factor is always re-normed, and only a genuine one-off closes with a waiver.

## Refutation

A reproducible cause of a difficulty whose governing norm was left unchanged and which nonetheless did not recur over many later occurrences of the activity would show the reproduction does not carry the failure.
