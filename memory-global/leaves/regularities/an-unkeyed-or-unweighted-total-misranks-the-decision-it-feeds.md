# A measurement counted in a unit other than the decision's (raw tokens for a cost decision), counting events more than once, or lacking a key to the thing decided about ranks the alternatives differently from the decision's real unit, however accurate its totals

## Regularity

A measurement whose unit is not the unit of the decision it feeds — raw tokens where the decision is about cost and token classes are priced differently — ranks the alternatives differently from that unit; one that counts an event in several places inflates what it counts; one whose observations carry no key to the thing the decision is about (the stage, the plan, the revision) cannot attribute its totals to any alternative. Each of these defects leaves the totals accurate while the decision drawn from them is wrong.

## Generality

Level 2 — ranges over measurements built to inform a choice. It is not claimed for a measurement kept only as a record with no decision attached.

## Refutation

A decision observed to rank its alternatives identically under the unweighted and the decision-unit measure, and under keyed and unkeyed observations, across many uses would show the misranking does not occur for that decision.
